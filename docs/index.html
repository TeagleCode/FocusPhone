Privacy Policy

FocusPhone (com.teaglecode.focusphone) · last updated 15 August 2026

FocusPhone does not collect, transmit, or sell any personal data. There are no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, and no third-party SDKs of any kind. Everything the app records stays in its own private storage on your phone.

What the app stores

All of it lives in Android's private per-app storage, readable only by FocusPhone. None of it leaves the device.

  • The apps you have blocked or time-limited, and their limits.
  • How long you have spent in those apps today, so limits can be enforced.
  • The in-app sections you have chosen to block, and the text and view-id hints used to detect them.
  • Your daily agenda items and whether they are complete.
  • Your chosen quote, font, colour and size.
  • A salted SHA-256 hash of your emergency code, if you set one. The code itself is never stored.
  • Your Anthropic API key, if you supply one for reading quizzes.

The accessibility service

Blocking is implemented with Android's AccessibilityService API. This is the only mechanism Android offers that can tell which app is in the foreground and close it, so the app cannot function without it.

The service is used for exactly three things:

  • Noticing when a restricted app comes to the foreground, and returning you to the home screen.
  • Counting the time you spend in a time-limited app.
  • Reading the on-screen view identifiers of apps you have chosen to section-block, so it can tell a short-form video feed from the rest of the app.

The service is scoped to the apps you have selected and cannot see any others. The list of packages it receives events from is set at runtime and enforced by Android itself, not by this app. Banking, messaging, email and every other app you have not explicitly restricted are invisible to it.

Screen content read by the service is examined in memory to make an immediate block decision and is then discarded. It is never written to storage, never logged, and never transmitted anywhere.

Usage access

If you grant usage access, the app reads Android's own usage statistics to make its time totals more accurate — specifically to recover time that passed while the accessibility service was off. These figures are used on the device to decide whether a limit has been reached, and are not transmitted.

The one network connection

FocusPhone makes no network requests at all unless you turn on the optional reading-quiz feature and supply your own Anthropic API key.

When you use that feature, the passage of the book you selected is sent to api.anthropic.com so that comprehension questions can be generated from it. That request goes to Anthropic under your own API key and is governed by Anthropic's privacy policy and commercial terms. No identifier of you, your device, or your other apps is included.

Your API key is stored on the device, is never displayed back in full, and is never sent anywhere other than Anthropic. If you never enter a key, the app never contacts the network.

Other apps on your device

As a home screen replacement, the app reads the list of apps that have a launcher icon, so it can draw the app list, launch them, and let you pick which ones to restrict. It does not request the broad QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES permission — it sees only apps you could already open from a home screen, and nothing else. That list is used on the device to draw the launcher and is never transmitted.

Permissions, in plain terms

  • Accessibility — blocks restricted apps and counts time. Scoped to the apps you choose.
  • Usage access (optional) — makes time totals accurate.
  • Package visibility — sees apps that have a launcher icon, so the home screen can list and open them.
  • Internet — used only for reading quizzes, only with your own API key.
  • Notifications — tells you when a limit has been reached.
  • Run at startup — restores your rules after a reboot.

Children

FocusPhone is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, including children.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app removes everything it has stored. There is no account, no server, and no copy of your data anywhere else, so there is nothing left behind and nothing to request the deletion of.
